## Verified from the form, unverified elsewhere

Verified (letter-of-intent form text): 10 fellowships; start 1 June 2027; 12 months; Track II is for "senior-level, independent project"; Track II issue areas include **"Hard infrastructure: compute, energy, quantum, and the extremes"** and **"Sovereign AI, public infrastructure, and maintenance"**; applicants confirm they are "legally able to receive fellowship and grant funding from Mozilla Foundation"; there is a charitable-purpose question and a location screen that can end the application; Kenya is in the country list; an AI and translation disclosure is required and "applications that appear to be heavily or entirely AI-generated ... may be subject to further review".

## Eligibility traps

- **Individual only.** Datacraft cannot apply. Applicant: you.
- **Charitable purpose.** The form asks if the project serves a charitable purpose and public benefit "beyond the users". Open-source planning tools for public-interest grid decisions can answer yes; a product for Datacraft's customers cannot. The draft says so and so must the plan.
- **Work in the open.** The form scores it. ALEA is open.
- **Track II is "senior-level".** The form asks years of professional experience (bands up to 16+). Answer truthfully.
- **Location screen.** If a hard-stop flag is triggered by country, the form ends. Run it to find out before December.
- **AI disclosure.** The form asks "Were AI tools used to complete this application?" and how. Mozilla says AI should be "supportive functions, not to generate full responses". This draft is a scaffold. The Mozilla answers are personal (role in the field, motivation, what you want from a cohort) and must be yours; the draft leaves those to you on purpose.

## Fit, and the risk

Mozilla wants "builders" working on "technology that's too early, too unexpected or too ambitious for anyone else to fund", with a posture on "who technology should serve". The fit is a public, auditable alternative to opaque energy planning models, owned by African planners. The risk: ALEA's graph is a dependency graph, the solver coupling is 7.4%, and the work is mid-build; a reviewer who expects traction numbers will find few. The form asks for traction and says pre-launch applicants should say what they are measuring. The draft does.
