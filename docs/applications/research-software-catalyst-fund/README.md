## Eligibility, checked against the FAQ

- Individuals, informal teams, institutions and non-profits, anywhere, unless US sanctions law blocks payment. Kenya is not under comprehensive sanctions.
- **Not LLCs, companies or other for-profits.** Datacraft cannot apply. Apply as an individual; Hack Club Bank pays individuals.
- Must not be "work that mainly benefits a private company or individual rather than the research community". The proposal therefore centres on the reusable method and the open outputs, not on ALEA's commercial prospects.
- No policy or advocacy work.
- Software under an OSI licence (ALEA is MIT). Documents, data and playbooks under CC BY or CC0. Code on GitHub, documents and data archived on Zenodo.
- Unlikely to be funded: a new package for one project with no wider practice outcome; compute or licences as the substance; work with no public output. The proposal is framed to avoid the first of these.
- End of grant: a public output and a short note on what you learned. No long reporting.
- They cannot give pre-submission feedback on scope.

## Fit, and the risk

The fund wants "trustworthy research software with AI". ALEA's honest fit: it was built largely by an AI assistant, and the project has a recorded method for catching what that produces (failing-on-purpose gates, a dimension checker, a register of unverified claims, archived sources). The reviewable output is that method as a template and a playbook with real defect case studies. The risk is that a reviewer reads it as "a new package for one project". The draft answers by making the template and playbook the deliverable and the ALEA fixes the worked example.
