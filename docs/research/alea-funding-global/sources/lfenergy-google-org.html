    LF Energy Receives $2.5 Million in Funding from Google.org via INCO to Advance Open Source Tools for Europe's Power Grids

    The Linux Foundation | 10 November 2026

    Two-year program will strengthen open source grid modeling, forecasting and data projects, helping more European grid operators put them to use 

    Summary

    • LF Energy and Linux Foundation Europe will deploy $2.5 million in funding from Google.org via INCO to advance open source modeling, forecasting and data tools for Europe's electricity grids.
    • The two-year investment will support development across four of LF Energy’s core open source grid software and data projects (OpenSynth, PowSyBl, Power Grid Model and OpenSTEF) to improve access to AI-ready grid data, accelerating power system modeling and strengthening energy forecasting.
    • Funding will also support training, support resources and adoption programs to help grid operators across Europe deploy these tools, with a focus on making advanced digital capabilities accessible to smaller and less-resourced organizations.

    VIENNA – Nov. 10, 2026 – Enlit Europe – LF Energy, the Linux Foundation's neutral home to build the digital foundation for energy together through open source software, standards and data, today announced $2.5 million in funding from Google.org administered via INCO to accelerate the development and adoption of open source tools for Europe's electricity grids. Awarded to Linux Foundation Europe and deployed with LF Energy over two years, the funding will will advance AI-ready grid data, faster power system modeling and more accurate energy forecasting through four LF Energy projects:

    • OpenSynth: An open data community that gives researchers and grid planners realistic energy data without exposing customer information, so they can study how heat pumps, electric vehicles (EVs) and solar will affect local grids. It includes 10 million AI-generated synthetic household load profiles and real French transmission grid topology published by RTE.
    • PowSyBl: A modular framework for transmission grid analysis that helps operators manage congestion and plan grid reinforcements and new renewable connections. RTE in France and TenneT in the Netherlands use it in production, and TenneT built a PowSyBl-based solution in five months that improved calculation performance tenfold.
    • Power Grid Model: A high-performance calculation engine for distribution grids that runs thousands of scenarios at once, so operators can see where growth in EVs, heat pumps and solar will overload local networks and plan ahead. It has passed 10 million downloads and is used and developed by Dutch distribution operators Alliander, Enexis and Stedin.
    • OpenSTEF: A machine learning framework that forecasts electricity load and generation from minutes to days ahead, giving operators time to head off congestion and put spare grid capacity to use for flexible loads, such as EV charging. Alliander uses it to forecast grid congestion and Sigholm uses it to plan district heating production.

    Europe's electricity grids are being asked to carry more renewable generation, electrified heating and transport and data center demand than they were built for, and new lines cannot be permitted and built quickly enough to keep pace. In the near term, grid operators must maximize use of the capacity they already have, which depends on technology to observe, model and forecast their networks. Rather than building new technology from scratch, this funding will help further develop existing open source solutions with AI to address local grid needs and drive adoption across Europe, especially for smaller and lower-resourced operators and regions.

    "AI will only be as useful to grid operators as the data and models it is built on," said Alex Thornton, executive director of LF Energy. "This grant from Google.org via INCO invests in that foundation: open data, grid models and forecasting tools that operators across Europe can build on. Because the work is open source, its benefits reach every operator, not only those with the resources to develop these capabilities themselves."

    “Supporting a reliable, resilient power grid requires not just new physical infrastructure, but open digital tools that help operators get more out of the grid capacity we already have. Through this $2.5 million in Google.org funding to Linux Foundation Europe, we’re proud to support LF Energy in scaling open source data, modeling and AI forecasting tools—helping grid operators of every size across Europe modernise grid planning and unlock more affordable, reliable, and clean energy for everyone,” said Adam Elman, Director of Sustainability, EMEA at Google.

    Beyond technical development, the funding will support technical guides and implementation resources, training courses, workshops co-located with LF Energy Summit Europe and translations into various EU languages. Grid operators and other organizations interested in participating can contact info@lfenergy.org. To learn more about LF Energy and its open source projects, visit lfenergy.org.

    About LF Energy

    LF Energy is the Linux Foundation’s neutral home to build the digital foundation for energy together. We host more than three dozen open source projects used to plan, operate, and modernize energy systems. The portfolio spans from transmission system modeling to substation virtualization, smart meter integration to EV charging. We provide the trust layer: neutral governance, legal frameworks, security practices, and community of practitioners that turn open source software, standards, and data into infrastructure the industry can depend on for decades. Members include utilities, energy companies, vendors, researchers, and technology providers, aligned to deliver affordable, reliable, safe, and clean energy. For more information, please visit lfenergy.org.

    About The Linux Foundation

    The Linux Foundation is the world’s leading home for collaboration on open source software, hardware, standards, and data. Linux Foundation projects, including Linux, Kubernetes, Model Context Protocol (MCP), OpenChain, OpenSearch, OpenSSF, OpenStack, PyTorch, Ray, RISC-V, SPDX and Zephyr, provide the foundation for global infrastructure. The Linux Foundation is focused on leveraging best practices and addressing the needs of contributors, users, and solution providers to create sustainable models for open collaboration.
