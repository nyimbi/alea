 Call For Applications: Climate Intervention Environmental Impact Fund 2026 - MSME Africa Connect with us Share Tweet Share The Climate Intervention Environmental Impact Fund (CIEIF) is inviting applications from investigators and organisations developing innovative climate-intervention technologies that are approaching small-scale field testing. The fund will award three grants of $75,000 each in its sixth grant round. Funding will support environmental impact assessments, predictive impact modelling and stakeholder engagement linked to proposed climate-intervention field tests. The application deadline is December 1, 2026 . What Is the CIEIF Grant? The Climate Intervention Environmental Impact Fund supports projects that aim to address the root causes of global warming or its major effects through innovative climate-intervention technologies. Rather than funding the development or general implementation of a climate project, CIEIF provides grants for the analysis and stakeholder engagement needed to assess the potential environmental, social, economic and health impacts of a proposed field test. Advertisement The current funding round will make three awards of $75,000 each . What Can the $75,000 Grant Fund? Applicants must show how the funding will be used primarily for at least one of three areas: Environmental impact assessment (EIA) Predictive environmental impact modelling Engagement with potentially affected stakeholders , including communities and government agencies The grant can cover relevant consultant costs, applicant staff time and closely associated expenses connected to this work. Selected applicants will receive the first third of the grant after discussions with CIEIF about the expert consulting needs of their proposed field test. The remaining payments will follow an agreed schedule linked to project milestones. Who Can Apply? CIEIF accepts applications from qualified investigators and organisations developing innovative climate-intervention technologies that are approaching field testing. The fund has a global reach and can consider applications from private businesses. However, it gives higher priority to nonprofit institutions, including academic institutions . International projects may receive higher priority where a US nonprofit partner is available to accept the grant as a donation. Advertisement Individuals and student projects are not eligible. What Types of Climate Projects Are Eligible? CIEIF considers innovative interventions that could potentially be scaled to deliver meaningful climate benefits. Examples listed by the fund include: Biochar Enhanced weathering Carbon dioxide removal methods other than Direct Air Capture Cloud brightening Ocean interventions and marine carbon removal Methane removal Livestock methane capture Agricultural climate interventions Ice and land albedo enhancement Greenhouse-gas-removing coatings Black soot removal or mitigation Nitrous oxide and chlorofluorocarbon removal Other similar climate-intervention technologies The proposed intervention must be approaching a small-scale field test , rather than remaining at the basic research stage. What Projects Are Not Eligible? CIEIF does not provide general implementation or basic research funding. Projects focused primarily on areas such as: Advertisement Climate adaptation Clean energy Climate resilience General emissions reduction Sustainable development are outside the fund’s scope unless the project involves an eligible climate intervention that addresses a root cause of global warming or its major effects. The fund also excludes stratospheric or higher solar radiation management , as well as Carbon Capture Utilization and Storage (CCUS) and Direct Air Capture (DAC) . What Must Be Included in the Application? There is no standard application form or template . Applicants should prepare a grant request of no more than 20 single-spaced pages , using 12-point font and written in English. Biosketches and important scientific publications can be attached as supplementary material. The application should address: Contact details and biosketches of the project personnel. A detailed description of the proposed field test and evidence that the technology is ready for testing. Expected technical and environmental challenges. Potential economic, social, public health and safety impacts. Required government permits and approvals. Previous publications relating to the technology. At least two independent consulting firms that could support the impact assessment, modelling or stakeholder-related work. A proposed budget showing how the $75,000 would be used. Other sources of funding for the investigation. Relevant insurance arrangements. Confirmation that the project’s results can be published. Any other important information about the proposed field test. Important Application Requirement CIEIF has specifically stated that AI-generated content cannot be used in cover letters or applications . The organisation says applications will be reviewed for AI use and that applications identified as using AI-generated information will be rejected, with applicants potentially barred from future applications. Advertisement For this reason, applicants should prepare their submissions themselves and follow CIEIF’s instructions carefully. How to Apply There is no standard online application form. Once the grant request is ready, applicants should contact Peter T. Jenkins, Manager, CIEIF, at [email protected] . CIEIF will then discuss the appropriate way to submit the application. The fund says applications will be kept confidential. The initiative is implemented by Uganda’s Ministry of Gender, Labour and Social Development with support from the World Bank and the Private Sector Foundation Uganda. Grant Amount Under the programme, funding is divided according to business size. Micro enterprises can receive grants ranging from UGX 3.5 million to UGX 38 million, while small and medium-sized enterprises with up to 49 employees can access between UGX 37 million and UGX 111 million. Advertisement The government has clarified that the funding is a grant and does not have to be repaid. Successful businesses may use the support to acquire machinery and equipment, increase production, digitise operations, develop new products, improve business processes and access new markets. Eligible Business Sectors The competition targets women-owned enterprises operating in sectors where women’s participation remains relatively low. Eligible sectors include: Agribusiness and agricultural value addition Green businesses and renewable energy Construction and related technical services Manufacturing Arts and creative industries Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics (STEM) Information and Communications Technology (ICT) Social enterprises and childcare services Proposed business activities must demonstrate potential to improve productivity, support innovation, create employment or expand market access. Eligibility Requirements To qualify, applicants must meet the following conditions: Advertisement The enterprise must be registered or legally recognised in Uganda. Women must own more than 50 per cent of the business. The enterprise must be managed and controlled by women. The business must have operated for at least one year. It must have a verifiable physical business location in Uganda. Applicants must provide evidence of business ownership, registration and operations. The opportunity is open to eligible Ugandan women entrepreneurs and legally registered refugee women operating businesses in the country. Required Documents Applicants should prepare their identification documents, business registration certificates or relevant trading licences, evidence of women’s ownership and records showing business operations. The programme also requests financial records, including bank statements covering at least 12 months or mobile money statements covering at least six months. Applicants should review their submissions carefully and ensure they receive confirmation after completing the process.
