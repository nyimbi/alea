The Use Cases

We’re looking for teams ready to put their GenAI expertise to work — where cities need it most. This Challenge centers on three real-world, city-owned use cases across Europe and Central Asia, each shaped by a partner city’s own priorities within the green and digital transition.

Modernizing Transit Route Reviews

City: Pristina, Kosovo1

Pristina is growing fast, and its bus routes need regular review to keep pace. This use case explores a multilingual GenAI assistant that helps municipal planners pull together transport routes, traffic data, and residents’ feedback so that the public transit system can adapt as the city grows.

View Use Case

Unlocking Energy Efficiency Investments

City: Rivne, Ukraine

Residents, homeowner associations and small businesses in Rivne want to cut costs and build energy resilience, but don’t know where to start. This use case explores a GenAI-powered energy efficiency assistant that walks users from a building profile to indicative costs, financing options, and a ready-to-share report.

View Use Case

Safeguarding Green Infrastructure

City: Skopje, North Macedonia

Skopje’s digital Green Cadastre keeps track of the city’s green infrastructure, including trees and green spaces. However, the records behind it are scattered across formats and departments. This use case explores how GenAI could connect those records to the Cadastre to support decision-making, ultimately safeguarding the city’s green infrastructure.

View Use Case

1 References to Kosovo shall be understood to be in the context of Security Council resolution 1244 (1999).

Applicant Resources

  • Eligibility & Contest Rules

    View Full Contest Rules HERE >>
    • Platform Submission
      Applicants must complete all mandatory Phase 1 submissions on both the Funding Portal and Zindi platforms
    • Use Case Alignment
      Proposed solutions must directly address a designated municipal use case in Pristina, Rivne, or Skopje.
    • 1- Year Commitment
      In accordance with the official Terms and Conditions, teams are required to commit to participating across all stages of the competition throughout the coming year, including the sprint, prototyping, and piloting phases.
    • Team Composition
      Teams must be informal groups of at least two (2) individuals (corporate entities or organizations are ineligible). Every team member must be 18 years of age or older.
    • Geographic Eligibility 
      The Challenge is open to individuals globally. Please read the Contest Rules for full eligibility details.
    • Open-Source Licensing
      Teams must agree to release all project code, data, documentation, and models under an approved permissive open-source license (e.g., (Apache 2.0, MIT , or equivalent) without seeking personal financial gain or patent rights.
    • GENIE. AI
      GENIE Al is the open-source framework that makes this possible, and all teams are required to use it, and you’ll only need to explain how you plan to incorporate it in your narrative proposal (the technical assessment does not require the use of GENIE AI).     • Technical Assessment
      A practical skills evaluation hosted on Zindi, a data science challenge platform used by Al practitioners around the world. You’ll create a free Zindi account, complete a hands-on task, and your results will help reviewers understand vour team’s ability to bring your solution to life
      Access Zindi >>
    Phases & Evaluation Criteria

    Phase 1 (Open Call):

    Technical Skills Assessment:
    Candidates are filtered by performance through a skills assessment on the Zindi Platform. The top 100 technical performers move to panel evaluation

    Narrative Submission Assessment
    A panel reviews narrative submissions using the following core criteria:

    • Use Case Alignment (20%): The application demonstrates a clear understanding of the city use case and showing how the proposed solution directly addresses its key challenges.
    • Impact Potential (25%): The application demonstrates expected value and outcome of deploying the solution using a responsible, inclusive, and open-source design.
    • Team Capability (20%): The application demonstrates technical expertise, related experience, and readiness to collaborate with the use case owner.
    • Technical Approach (35%): The application demonstrates the technical feasibility of the proposed solution, its innovative methodology, and the appropriate use of data and technology, including how the team plans to incorporate GENIE AI.

    Phase 2 (Semi-Finalists):
    Reviewers will evaluate the updated narrative proposals, reflecting key insights and expertise gained during the Use Case Sprint, using the core evaluation categories listed in Phase 1 (Technical Approach, Impact Potential, Use Case Alignment, and Team Capability).

    Phase 3 (Finalists):
    Reviewers will evaluate the prototypes and narrative applications and live pitch presentation on the core evaluation categories listed in Phase 1 (Technical Approach, Impact Potential, Use Case Alignment, and Team Capability).
