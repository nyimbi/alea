Questions

Frequently asked questions

If your question is not here, email us.

Does my idea have to match one of the workshop's areas?

No. The Edinburgh workshop report and Reflections from Edinburgh show what the community has already prioritised, and the ADSA and US-RSE position statement gives the wider context. They are there to orient you, not to constrain you.

Do I need to have been at the Edinburgh workshop?

No. The workshop shaped the thinking, but the call is open. Proposals from people with no connection to it are read on exactly the same terms.

Are there any restrictions on what I can apply for?

A few. While we aim to support a broad range of activities, we cannot support policy or advocacy work. For a sense of what we can fund, see the examples and the full list of what we cannot fund.

Can you give me feedback on my idea before I submit?

Unfortunately not. We cannot review ideas or draft proposals, or tell you ahead of time whether something is in scope or likely to be funded. Every proposal is read by the review panel on the same terms, so we cannot give anyone an early view. Use what we fund and the examples to judge fit, and if you think your idea fits, apply.

Do I need an institution to receive the money?

No. The fund is fiscally hosted at Hack Club Bank, which can pay individuals and unincorporated groups as well as institutions.

Can a company or other for-profit organisation apply?

No. The fund cannot make grants to LLCs, companies, or other for-profit organisations. Individuals, informal teams, institutions, and non-profits can apply.

Can I apply from outside the United States?

Yes. Applications are welcome worldwide, and the fund can make international payments. The exception is where US sanctions law prevents a payment, such as to people or organisations in countries or regions under comprehensive US sanctions.

How much should I ask for?

Ask for what the work costs, up to $50,000. Most exploratory grants are expected to land between $15,000 and $50,000.

Can I include travel?

Yes. Travel to bring a distributed team together for a sprint or workshop is a legitimate cost.

Can I include overheads?

Yes, up to 10% of the total budget for indirect costs. The rest should cover the direct costs of the proposed work. The $50,000 maximum includes any indirect costs.

Can I submit more than one proposal?

There is a single deadline, so put your strongest idea forward. If you are involved in two related proposals, tell us in both.

Who reviews the proposals?

A panel of five to seven community members. Anyone with a proposal under review does not serve on the panel.

How long until I hear back?

By mid-March 2027. The deadline is December 31, 2026, and proposals are reviewed from January to March 2027.

What happens if my proposal is declined?

You will be notified of the decision, but we cannot provide individual feedback on proposals. Some declined proposals may be invited to revise for the impact fund when it opens.

Will I receive individual feedback on my proposal?

No. We cannot provide individual feedback on proposals, either before or after you submit. See feedback before you submit.

When does the impact fund open?

In 2027. It is not open yet, and there is nothing to submit for it. Details will be published on this site.

What do you expect at the end of a grant?

A public output (a tool, a curriculum, a dataset, a document, a report of what happened) and a short note on what you learned. There is no long reporting burden.

How do outputs have to be licensed?

Openly. Software must be open source under an OSI-approved license, and non-software outputs (documents, curricula, playbooks, data) must be released under CC BY or CC0. Outputs should also live somewhere publicly accessible: code on a platform such as GitHub or GitLab, and documents, data, and other files archived on a service such as Zenodo. The intent is that everything the fund pays for enters the commons, free for anyone to use and build on.

Can I join the fund as a funder?

Yes. We welcome conversations with prospective funders who want to support trustworthy AI-assisted research software. Email the programme to discuss joining the fund. Tell us a little about yourself or your organisation and what you would like to support.
