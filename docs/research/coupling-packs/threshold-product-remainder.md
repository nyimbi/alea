# Coupling pack: the THRESHOLD class and the PRODUCT remainder

## What this pack covers

This pack proposes couplings for the two classes the worklist names as the
remainder after batch 28: the 25 **THRESHOLD** nodes (capacity bounds, prudential
floors and two time ceilings) and the 41 **PRODUCT** nodes of the worklist's
four groups (five placeholders, five technology capex/opex bases, twenty indices
and multipliers with no consumer, eleven densities and site counts). Sixty-six
rows, one per node. The THRESHOLD mechanism is not the PRODUCT mechanism and the
two sections say so separately: an inequality cannot determine its left-hand
side — `Equation._a_derivation_has_exactly_one_target` refuses
`determined_variables` on a non-`eq` relation — so a THRESHOLD node couples only
by being *named* on the left of a bound, and its role stays `free`. That gives
the THRESHOLD class a structural result this pack states once and does not repeat
twenty-five times: **no THRESHOLD node can be derived, and the class contributes
zero derived nodes to any batch.** A PRODUCT node, by contrast, either becomes
the left-hand side of an equality or is read as an operand of one.

Two things were verified against the tree rather than assumed. First, the graph
this pack reads is *ahead* of the worklist: `uncoupled()` holds **365** nodes
against the batch-28 bake's 422, because `config/prg.yaml` carries a large
uncommitted batch (1,371 insertions, 318 deletions at the time of writing).
**Six of the 66 slice nodes are already coupled by equations in that working
tree** — five PRODUCT (`archaeological_sites_density`, `sacred_sites_density`,
`synthetic_fuel_share_aviation`, `synthetic_fuel_share_shipping`,
`distribution_network_km`) and one THRESHOLD (`hydrogen_storage_capacity`) — and
their rows record the equation that closed them rather than proposing a second
one. Second, the worklist's headline "42 PRODUCT" is one more than its four
groups enumerate: the groups name 41 nodes, and this pack covers those 41.

Every proposed right-hand side was run through the BRIEF's symbol check in one
pass over the table as written: **60 equations, zero unresolved symbols**, once
the 21 new left-hand-side ids are allowed for; no target appears on its own
right-hand side, and none of the 21 collides with a declared id. Each right-hand
side was also evaluated at the propagated defaults, and the values quoted in the
rows below are the printed ones, not estimates. Every right-hand side was
additionally run through `units.unit_of`. On a THRESHOLD row the right-hand side
*is* the ceiling node, and the walker returns `None` for twenty of the
twenty-five because the ceiling is the node this pack retypes — the retype is a
precondition of the bound, not a preference, since `check_equation` reports
undecidable rather than inconsistent when one side has no unit. On a PRODUCT row
the walker resolves the consumer's arithmetic, and the row's prose states the
dimension it produced (people, USD, TWh/year, m3/s) rather than the unit of the
node being read.

### A note on the `kind` vocabulary

The BRIEF defines two kinds, `derive` and `read`. A THRESHOLD node is neither: it
is named on the left of an inequality and is not determined by it. This pack uses
a third kind, **`bound`**, meaning "the node is the left operand of an inequality
that is new or amended"; its role stays `free`, and its value is not computed by
the equation. Without that word the THRESHOLD section would have to call a
non-deriving left-hand side a `derive`, which is the mis-reporting the register
exists to prevent.

### How to read a THRESHOLD row

The bound's left-hand side is usually an existing endogenous build
(`coal_capacity` 3000 MW, `gas_capacity` 1500, `hydro_capacity` 1500,
`nuclear_capacity` 0, `solar_pv_capacity` 10000) and the THRESHOLD node is the
ceiling. The bound therefore holds at defaults whenever the ceiling's default
exceeds the build, and fails only in a scenario that grows the build. That is
what a capacity ceiling is for, and it is also why a ceiling whose *range* is a
superset of the build's own range is a tautology: it cannot fail at any
admissible value. Each row states which of the two it is.

A second structural fact applies to every bound over an endogenous build.
`constraints._classify` routes an equation to `bound` (a linopy constraint) only
when every symbol on *both* sides is a declared network quantity or a settable
scalar with a default; an endogenous build is neither, so **every ceiling whose
left-hand side is an endogenous fleet build routes `unbound`** — a post-solve
audit check, not a constraint on the solve. Measured on the 23 proposed bounds:
**12 route `bound`, 11 route `unbound`.** The eleven unbound are
`auction_price_cap` (its right-hand side, `carbon_price_escalated_usd_per_tco2`,
is derived), `coal_new_build`, `gas_new_build`, `hydro_new_build`,
`nuclear_new_build`, `solar_pv_land_constraint`, `solar_pv_max_build`,
`solar_thermal_capacity`, `storage_capacity_mw`, `thermal_storage_capacity` and
`transboundary_water_constraint`. A batch author who expects these to shape the
LP should know that before writing them; the twelve that do bind are the ones
stated over a settable scalar on both sides.

## Dependencies

Nodes and bases this pack needs that belong to another slice or another batch.
Each is named rather than proposed.

1. **`hydrogen_storage_duration`** (hours, new base) — blocks the
   `hydrogen_storage_capacity` bound. A hydrogen store's hours are not any of
   `battery_duration` (24), `phs_duration` (6) or `thermal_storage_duration` (8).
2. **`max_remedy_period_days`** (days, default 90) — blocks the `remedy_periods`
   bound. Without it the only rhs available is the constant 365, which restates
   the node's own range.
3. **`grievances_received_per_year`** (events/year) and its own base
   `jobs_per_grievance` (job-years per event) — block
   `grievance_redress_effectiveness`. `direct_jobs` exists, so the count is
   derivable once the per-grievance rate is a node.
4. **`capacity_factor_gas_ocgt`** (1, 0.3 per the archived IEA/NEA text) and a
   heat-rate term — block `capex_gas_ocgt`. The shipped LCOE form has no
   efficiency term, so at equal capacity factors the peaker prices below the
   combined cycle.
5. **`study_area_km2`** — the base the older classification pack wanted for
   `land_availability_fraction`. It is absent, and the shipped descriptions
   resolve the question the other way: `land_area_hectares` calls itself "the
   project's own land take", which is what an availability share applies to, so
   this pack does not need it. It stays listed because a pack that wants a study
   area for any other purpose will have to add it, and it cannot be derived from
   `land_area_hectares / land_availability_fraction` without making the share its
   own denominator.
6. **`annual_build` nodes** — `solar_pv_max_build` is described as an annual
   build ceiling but is stated against the PV *stock*, because no annual-increment
   node exists. If the annual reading is the intent, the increment is a base this
   pack does not propose.
7. **The BASE-NEEDED pack's `demand_response` share** — if that pack introduces a
   second flexible-share base, it must not also derive
   `demand_response_potential`: the alias in this pack claims the quantity is
   `interruptible_load_share`, and two bases for one share is the duplicate class
   this register exists to close.

## Rejected

The three prior rejections the worklist carries, re-verified rather than
repeated:

- **`line_outage_start` / `line_outage_end`** — confirmed. `docs/prg-status.md:978-995`
  records the pypsa probe on 1.4.0: a dynamic `lines_t.active` is accepted,
  stored, never read, and the solve sends 50 MW through the snapshots marked
  inactive. The honoured spelling is `lines_t.s_max_pu`, which is a rating, not a
  window. The nodes are also untyped timestamps, so the difference of the two has
  no dimension the walker can compute.
- **`storage_duration_curve`** — confirmed, and on the stronger ground: its
  proposed left-hand side would be a function of `storage_capacity_mw`, which is
  endogenous (`settable=False`), so the equality would make a derived node a
  function of a variable the optimiser chooses.
- **`cross_border_trading`** — confirmed, and out of this slice's arithmetic
  entirely: the node carries `deprecated_by: cross_border_trade_allowed`, and
  `graph._deprecated_reach` refuses any equation or edge naming a deprecated node.
  An alias onto it is not available at any spelling.

Newly rejected in this pack, with the reason stated in the row:

- **Fleet-balance membership for the technology ceilings.** The classification
  pack's proposal puts each capacity under `total_capacity_mw +
  storage_headroom_mw`. That right-hand side is 15000 MW and each node's range
  runs to 1e6, so the bound admits every value a plan could plausibly carry; it
  is a tautology, not a constraint. The pack instead bounds the three
  storage-like technologies (`compressed_air_capacity`, `flywheel_capacity`,
  `gravity_storage_capacity`) by `storage_headroom_mw` (5000 MW) and the seven
  generation ones (`fusion_capacity`, `hydrogen_fuel_cell_capacity`,
  `marine_otec_capacity`, `marine_tidal_capacity`, `marine_wave_capacity`,
  `small_modular_reactor_capacity`, `tidal_range_capacity`) by
  `total_capacity_mw` (10000 MW) — both of which a scenario can violate.
- **`spare_parts_lead_time leq 365 / maintenance_frequency`.** Dimensionally
  wrong: the right-hand side resolves to `year / events`, and a lead time is a
  time.
- **`remedy_periods leq 365`.** The node's declared range is [1, 365]; the bound
  restates the range and can never fail.
- **`lcoe_gas_ccgt_usd_per_mwh` as a new derived node.** It reproduces
  `lcoe_gas_definition` term for term (87.82 USD/MWh at defaults), so it is a
  second spelling rather than a coupling.
- **`thin_capitalisation_limit geq debt_equity_ratio`.** Fails at the graph's own
  defaults (2.33 > 1.5), so the batch would ship a violating graph.
- **`storage_capacity_mw eq battery_capacity + phs_capacity`.** Correct
  arithmetic (500 MW) that would make an endogenous node derived; recorded as a
  finding instead (the shipped 800 is 300 MW above the two builds it names).
