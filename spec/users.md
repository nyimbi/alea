The Alea system represents a fundamental shift from a single-country, single-purpose tool to a **continental, multi-purpose decision-support platform**. It answers a far broader set of questions and serves a much wider range of users than COMPASS, which is narrowly focused on South African power market price projections.

### 🎯 What Predictions Will Alea Answer?

The system will generate predictions across four interconnected domains, moving from physical system outcomes to financial and strategic insights.

*   **Physical & Operational Predictions**: It will forecast the **optimal generation and transmission capacity mix** for every African country and power pool to 2060, including the least-cost expansion of solar, wind, geothermal, hydro, biomass, and marine energy. It will simulate **hourly dispatch and cross-border power flows**, identifying congestion and curtailment risks. It will also model **water-energy nexus** dynamics, predicting how droughts or changing hydrology affect hydropower output and thermal plant cooling.

*   **Market & Price Predictions**: It will produce **wholesale electricity price projections** for all 55 AU member states, not just South Africa. It will calculate **renewable capture prices** at the technology and country level, accounting for cannibalisation as VRE penetration grows. It will also generate **PPA benchmark curves** with adjustments for contract tenor, currency risk, and counterparty risk.

*   **Financial & Risk Predictions**: It will deliver **bankability metrics** (DSCR, project IRR, equity IRR) for generation and transmission projects under multiple scenarios. It will generate **risk metrics (VaR, CVaR)** for asset revenues and portfolios. It will also produce **efficient frontiers** showing the trade-off between system cost and risk, enabling risk-averse investment planning.

*   **Strategic & Policy Predictions**: It will model the **impact of carbon taxes, capacity markets, and renewable auctions** on prices and investment. It will simulate **market power and strategic bidding behaviour** using agent-based modelling. It will also assess **climate resilience** under CMIP6 scenarios (SSP1-2.6 to SSP5-8.5).

### 💼 What Utility Will It Provide?

The platform's utility derives from its **continental scope, open architecture, and analytical depth**.

*   **For Continental Planning**: It provides the analytical engine for the **African Single Electricity Market (AfSEM)** and the **Continental Power System Master Plan (CMP)** . It can identify optimal cross-border interconnectors, quantify the benefits of regional trade, and assess the impact of regulatory harmonisation.

*   **For National Planning**: It gives every AU member state, including those with limited data, a **world-class modelling capability**. It can produce national capacity expansion plans, evaluate policy instruments, and stress-test strategies against fuel price, demand, and climate uncertainty.

*   **For Project Development**: It provides **bankable price forecasts and PPA benchmarks** for any African market. Developers can use it to identify optimal sites, size storage, and structure contracts. The Kenya case study, for example, used PyPSA-Earth to show that geothermal is the least-cost expansion option and that limiting it to 2 GW would require 8 GW of additional solar and increase annual system costs by €600 million.

*   **For Investment & Finance**: It offers **rigorous risk quantification** and portfolio stress-testing. Investors can compare the economic and climate impact per dollar invested across different technologies and countries, as BII did in its six-country study combining dispatch simulations with Value of Lost Load (VoLL) economic modelling.

### 💰 Commercial Applications and How They Compare to COMPASS Users

COMPASS is a **South Africa-specific subscription report** designed for banks, investors, developers, traders, large power users, and policymakers. Its commercial applications are concentrated in the **South African power market**: supporting NAV/GAV calculations, benchmarking projections, and stress-testing assumptions for SAWEM prices, capture rates, and PPA benchmarks.

Alea serves the **same user archetypes but across all 55 AU member states**, and it does so as an **open platform rather than a closed report**. The table below compares the commercial applications.

### 🏢 How It Will Help Businesses, Investors, and Planners

*   **For Businesses (Large Energy Users)**: It will enable **continental energy procurement strategies**. A mining company operating in South Africa, Zambia, and the DRC can model its energy costs under different PPA structures and carbon tax scenarios in each jurisdiction. It can identify opportunities for **on-site generation, wheeling, and cross-border power purchases**.

*   **For Investors and Financiers**: It will provide **defensible, scenario-based price forecasts** for any African market, reducing the risk of stranded assets. It will offer **portfolio-level risk metrics (VaR, CVaR)** and **bankability assessments** under multiple futures. The BII study demonstrated how this analysis can sharpen investment strategy and strengthen policy dialogue with governments.

*   **For Planners and Policymakers**: It will offer a **transparent, peer-reviewable** basis for national and continental planning. It will support **CMP and AfSEM implementation** by modelling cross-border trade, regulatory harmonisation, and market coupling. It will also enable **climate-resilient planning** by integrating CMIP6 hydrological and temperature uncertainty into capacity expansion.

In essence, Alea transforms energy modelling from a **South African subscription service** into a **continental public good**. It gives every stakeholder in Africa's energy transition the analytical tools to make better, more resilient decisions—whether they are a business managing energy costs, an investor allocating capital, or a planner designing the continent's energy future.
